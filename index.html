Wanted: “Useless” Days

The day after a test; the day before a break; planned sub days; standardized testing days; Super Bowl Monday; some random Thursday where you just can’t.

We all lose a few periods a year, even in math, despite its generous and reasonable pacing. Gimme them days, and I’ll give your students math practice conjoined with literacy and research skills.

(Exponential) Loving: It’s What I Got

Bellringers

5 minutes

Perfect for skeptics and those intimidated by my raw animal magnetism. I’ll provide a peculiar history of an actual number suitable for projection and/or analog work. Fair warning: bellringers end on a question your students can answer if you co-teach a full lesson with me!

Case Studies

one period

We co-teach: I do shake my groove thang, you do whatever you’re comfortable doing (math, maybe?). Students use your curricular content to trace a claim back to its source.

Projects

several days

Probably best suited for Statistics, Discrete, and the weeks after AP exams; I understand that you folks prefer to avoid multiday engagements. My two favored ideas: Numbers in the Archive and a deep dive into AI and statistical claims.

How It Works

  • Your place or mine? I will come to you; you can also bring your class to the library, where we have room to spread out, move between stations, look out the windows at flora and fauna, and, perhaps, put real books in eager little hands.
  • Planning an absence? Hit me up after you’re done in Frontline and I’ll keep your class busy with something active.
  • You check the math; I run the show, but am happy to share the stage. Everything is readymade by default, with nothing to grade unless your gradebook feels fallow.

Why It Counts

  • Students explain their reasoning in words and model real-world situations per the NJGPA’s interest in Expressing Mathematical Reasoning and Modeling and Application.
  • Every case study is built on your course standards. The Algebra 1 sample practices units, linear equations and inequalities, and slope as a rate.
  • New Jersey’s funky new administrative code calls for information literacy “delivered by school library media specialists and/or classroom educators in partnership with school library media specialists.”
